CargoTwin

A shipment tracker that logs every tilt and shock, with a 3D digital twin that moves exactly like the real box.

View repository ↗Live demo ↗

In action

Once goods are sealed in a shipping container, nobody sees what happens to them until the doors open at the other end. If the cargo arrives damaged, there's usually no record of when it happened, where, or how hard the container was knocked around. CargoTwin is a small tracker that goes inside the container and keeps that record: it notices when the box is tipped too far or dropped, notes the time and place, and mirrors the container's movement live on a 3D model in the browser. When the shipment arrives, whoever receives it can check the log and decide whether the cargo needs inspecting before they sign for it.

The tracker itself is an ESP32. An MPU6050 reads tilt and acceleration, a NEO-6M module reads position, and every time the box tips past a threshold or takes a hard knock the device logs the event with a UTC timestamp and GPS fix. Telemetry goes out over MQTT at 20 Hz to a Mosquitto broker, which feeds two consumers: a browser dashboard over WebSocket, where a 3D twin of a 20 ft container rotates live with the real box, and a Flask backend that writes every packet to SQLite for the history view. Events are detected on the device itself and saved to its flash, so a shipment that loses power or signal mid-route still has its incident log when it arrives.

What it does

Digital twin

A 3D 20 ft container at real dimensions, rotated live from the MPU. A ghost outline marks level, a cone marks the tilt limit, and the container flashes red the moment its up-vector leaves that cone.

On-device event detection

Debounced TILT events (start and end, with duration and peak angle) and instant SHOCK events, written to LittleFS on the ESP32 so they survive a power loss.

Timestamps without GPS

GPS UTC time when there's a fix. Indoors, the device falls back to a synced clock and keeps counting from it, and every event records which time source it used.

Incident replay

Click any logged incident and a red ghost container appears on the twin in the exact orientation the box was in at that moment, while the map jumps to where it happened.

Integrity summary

Each shipment is graded Intact, Inspect cargo, or Likely damaged (a tilt of 60 degrees or more, or a shock of 4 g or more), with max tilt and max shock alongside, exportable as CSV for a report.

Persistent history

The Flask backend subscribes to the same MQTT topics, batches packets into SQLite in WAL mode, and serves tilt and g-force charts plus the GPS route.

Scope

This is a working prototype built on a small box standing in for a container, not a deployed logistics product: the broker runs on a laptop on the same network as the ESP32, and in the MQTT setup the dashboard is receive-only (threshold changes don't reach the device yet).
